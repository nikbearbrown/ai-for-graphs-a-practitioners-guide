Figure 3.5 — Magnitude channels and identity channels

Munzner's two families. Quantitative attributes belong on magnitude channels; categorical attributes belong on identity channels. Every channel-misuse error is a violation of one of these two rules.

Magnitude channels versus identity channels taxonomy Two-column infographic. Left column lists magnitude channels (position, length, area, luminance) with icons and example uses and misuses. Right column lists identity channels (hue, shape, texture) with icons and example uses and misuses. Beneath each column, a principle box.
Magnitude channels
suited to quantitative / ordered data — convey "more" and "less"
Effectiveness principle (Munzner)
Encode the most important attribute on the highest-ranked appropriate channel.
Identity channels
suited to categorical data — convey "different from" but not "greater than"
Expressiveness principle (Munzner)
The channel must be capable of expressing the attribute type. No quantity on hue.

Two principles, generating every chart-design rule that follows. The most common encoding error in visualization — quantity on hue — is an expressiveness violation. The mirror error — categorical labels arranged along a magnitude scale — is the same violation in the other direction.

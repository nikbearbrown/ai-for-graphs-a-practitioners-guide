Figure 3.4 — Nightingale's rose, radius-linear vs area-corrected

Florence Nightingale's 1858 polar-area chart of British military mortality, rendered twice. The published form scales radius with the data and amplifies winter wedges; the area-corrected form scales radius with √value and preserves area proportionality.

Radius ∝ value (published form)
winter wedges visually dominate — area grows as the square of radius
Radius ∝ √value (area-honest)
each wedge's area is proportional to the data value

Nightingale knew the form amplified mortality. She used the amplification anyway, and Parliament funded sanitary reform. The lesson is not that polar area charts are bad — it is that distortion is a tool, and an honest designer knows when to use it.

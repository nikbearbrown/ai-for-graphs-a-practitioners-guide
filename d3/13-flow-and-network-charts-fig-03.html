Figure 13.3 — Three magnitude-family forms

Sankey, alluvial, ribbon chord. Each uses width as the magnitude channel. Each answers a different structural question.

A. Sankey
Q: how much flows A → B → C?
Left-to-right, directional. 5–30 nodes, 2–4 columns.
B. Alluvial
Q: how did categories shift across time?
Longitudinal categorical. 3–6 time points typical.
C. Ribbon chord
Q: how much flows between any pair?
Circular, symmetric. 6–20 entities.

Width ranks third in Cleveland and McGill. Less accurate than position; more accurate than area or color intensity. Choose the form by question structure.

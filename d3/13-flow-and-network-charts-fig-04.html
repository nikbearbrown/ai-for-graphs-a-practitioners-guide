Figure 13.4 — Four hairball mitigations

Same 200-node dense network. Filter, cluster, aggregate, or switch to a matrix. Each reveals one structural feature and hides another.

1. Unmitigated force-directed
No structure visible. The hairball.
Reveals: a network exists. Gives up: every structural question.
2. Filter — top 30 by degree
Sparse; structure becomes visible.
Reveals: hub-and-spoke. Gives up: filtered nodes and edges.
3. Cluster — eight super-nodes
Community structure visible; nodes hidden.
Reveals: community structure. Gives up: every individual node identity.
4. Adjacency matrix — sorted by degree
Every connection visible; spatial layout gone.
Reveals: full connection set; degree blocks. Gives up: cluster proximity.

Pick the mitigation whose loss you can afford. The hairball is not a design failure — it is a structural failure of single-level visualization on dense data.

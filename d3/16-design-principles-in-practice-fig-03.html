Figure 16.3 — Three color vocabularies

Three data types, three palette families. Using the wrong one introduces false order or hides true structure.

A. Categorical
unordered categories
Five product lines, no inherent order
Distinct hues, matched luminance. No category reads as more important by virtue of being darker or brighter. Limit to seven.
ColorBrewer qualitative · color-blind safe
B. Sequential
ordered or quantitative
Income by county
Single hue, varying luminance. Pale = low, dark = high. Reversing the convention costs the reader without warning.
viridis · magma · grays
C. Diverging
centered on a midpoint
Budget variance from target
Two hues meeting at a meaningful zero, darker as values diverge. If no real midpoint exists, use sequential instead.
RdBu · BrBG · PRGn

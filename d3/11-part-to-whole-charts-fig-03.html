Figure 11.3 — Nightingale polar area chart, with the distortion named

A simplified 1854–55 mortality dataset rendered as Nightingale rendered it — and labeled for what it does.

Wedge area encodes value
Area scales as the square of the radial length. A wedge twice as long appears roughly four times larger in area. The eye reads area; the data is the radial length.
Defensible in advocacy
Nightingale's chart argued for sanitary reform. The visual force served the argument. In an analytical context, the same distortion is a failure unless disclosed.

A polar area chart is a known distortion. The conditions that make the choice defensible are specific — Cairo's rhetorical-vs-analytical frame names them.
